I've built the NEO Orrery: an interactive 3D view of the Sun, planets and all 42,075 near-Earth asteroids, plus every direction in the plan. A clean `pnpm install && pnpm build` succeeds and outputs a self-contained static site in `dist/` with relative URLs. `data/` is untouched. **How it works**
- **Positions:** each body's position is computed from its own epoch with two-body Kepler motion (`src/orbit.ts`). Closed orbits use Kepler's equation, hyperbolic comets the hyperbolic form, and parabolic comets Barker's equation from perihelion time. As a check, Earth comes out at 179.6° ecliptic longitude at the 2026 March equinox; it should be about 180°.
- **Data step:** `scripts/prepare-data.ts` runs during the build. It turns `data/` into compact JSON in `dist/data/` (16 MB of asteroids becomes 6 MB) and links the impact-risk and close-approach rows to their asteroids. The app loads these files with `fetch` at runtime.
- **Stack:** Vite, TypeScript and three.js, no UI framework. The 42k objects are drawn as one point cloud, and hidden objects aren't computed.

**Features**
- **Time:** play, reverse, speeds from 1 hour/s to 10 years/s, a 1900–2200 scrubber, a date picker and a Today button.
- **Colour by:** hazard, orbit class, size, closest distance to Earth's orbit (MOID), or Palermo impact-risk scale, each with a legend.
- **Filters:** hazard status, orbit class, minimum diameter, maximum MOID, and "has a close approach within ±N days of the current date".
- **Search** across asteroids, comets and planets.
- **Highlights tab:** next Earth close approaches (clicking one jumps there and frames Earth), highest impact risk, closest recorded approaches, and largest potentially hazardous asteroids.
- **Detail panel:**
  - live distance to the Sun and Earth, and speed
  - orbit and physical data, with a size comparison ("about the size of the Eiffel Tower")
  - impact-risk figures
  - a clickable close-approach table, with the same dates marked on the timeline
- **Comets:** a layer you can switch on. It shows periodic comets only, or all of them including open orbits.
- **Follow mode, deep links and context:** the camera can lock onto a body as time runs. Time, selection, follow, camera and colour mode are kept in the URL, with a Copy link button. Distance rings, a scale bar that switches between au, km and lunar distances (LD), and orbit lines for up to 1,500 shown asteroids help with scale.

**Limitations**
- **Positions drift from reality decades from the data's date.** The two-body model ignores pull from the planets. For Apophis on 13 April 2029 the model shows 2.5 LD, against the real 0.1 LD. The close-approach tables come from JPL's full calculation and are the numbers to trust. The detail panel shows both and the help text explains the difference.
- **Diameters are often estimated.** Most asteroids have no measured diameter, so size is estimated from brightness assuming an albedo of 0.14. Estimated values are marked with "~".

The tight cluster of comets you'll see when comets are on is real data: 78 fragments of comet 73P/Schwassmann–Wachmann on nearly the same orbit.
