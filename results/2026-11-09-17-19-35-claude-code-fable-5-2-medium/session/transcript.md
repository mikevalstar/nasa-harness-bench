# Unslop

Edit text to remove AI patterns and add human voice.

## Process

1. Scan for the patterns below.
2. Rewrite. Preserve meaning, match intended tone.
3. Add soul (see next section).
4. Self-audit: "What makes this obviously AI generated?" Fix remaining tells.

## Adding soul

Removing patterns is half the job. Sterile, voiceless writing is just as obvious.

- **Have opinions.** React to facts instead of neutrally listing pros and cons.
- **Vary rhythm.** Short sentences. Then longer ones that take their time. Mix it up.
- **Acknowledge complexity.** "Impressive but also kind of unsettling" beats "impressive."
- **Use "I" when it fits.** First person isn't unprofessional.
- **Let some mess in.** Perfect structure looks machine-made.
- **Be specific.** Not "this is concerning" but "there's something unsettling about agents churning away at 3am."

## Patterns to detect and fix

1. **Puffery.** "pivotal moment", "testament to", "evolving landscape", "setting the stage for", "indelible mark", "deeply rooted". Cut puffery, state what happened.

3. **Superficial -ing phrases.** "highlighting...", "ensuring...", "reflecting...", "showcasing...", "fostering...". Delete or expand with real sources.

6. **Formulaic challenges.** "Despite challenges... continues to thrive." Replace with specific facts.

# Data Visualization

A chart is **read by people and executed by you**. This skill turns "make it look
good" into a procedure with checks, so the result is right by construction rather
than by taste.

**The method here is design-system-agnostic.** Nothing in the procedure, the form
heuristic, the six checks, or the mark specs is specific to one product. A design
system supplies a small set of *parameters* (its ramps, a categorical order, a
diverging pair, a status palette, a texture, its surfaces, its filter components);
the method consumes them unchanged. A **validated default palette** is the
reference instance, fully specified in `references/palette.md`. To target your
brand, read that file's structure and substitute its values - touch nothing else.

> The single most important habit: **the color part is computable, so compute it.**
> Never eyeball whether a palette is colorblind-safe - run `scripts/validate_palette.js`.

Color comes LAST. Most bad charts pick colors first.

1. **Pick the form.** What is the data's job - magnitude, identity, polarity, a
   single headline, change-over-time? The job picks the chart type, and sometimes
   the answer is *not a chart* (a stat tile or hero number). -> `references/choosing-a-form.md`
2. **Assign color by the job it does.** Categorical (identity), sequential

Things that behave differently from what you might assume:

- **Positions drift away from the epoch.** Everything is two-body motion from four-digit elements. Apophis in April 2029 lands 2.5 lunar distances from Earth in the scene, against a recorded 0.1. The detail panel shows the recorded figures; the scene shows the computed ones.
- **Distant comets fade out.** Otherwise the Kreutz sungrazers, all leaving along one line about 25 au out, read as a clump next to the Sun. Zooming out brings them back.
- **208 comets appear once, as asteroids.** They are in both `comets.json` and `asteroids.json`, so I dropped them from the comet overlay and they show under the "Comet-like" class.
- **Search ignores filters.** Filters change the scene and list together; search covers every asteroid, comet and planet.
- **Deep links don't carry filters.** The hash holds date, selection, follow state, camera and colour mode.

The README lists these and the other assumptions (diameter estimates from H, minimum on-screen planet sizes, the 1.5-day flyby marker window).
